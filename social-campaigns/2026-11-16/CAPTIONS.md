## Instagram caption
Local SEO service-area pages should help a nearby customer decide—not just repeat a city name.

If you run a US home-service business across several towns, resist the urge to duplicate one page and swap the location name.

Use this quick page test:
• Service: what specific job can someone request?
• Coverage: do you genuinely serve this place?
• Local value: what useful detail is actually different here—availability, access, service scope or process? Include only what is true.
• Proof: show real, permissioned work or verifiable business details. Never fill the gap with invented local claims.
• Next step: make it clear how someone can request service.

Google identifies substantially similar city or region pages that funnel visitors to one destination as a form of doorway abuse. A location page should stand on its own because it helps the visitor—not just because it contains another town name.

Choose one existing page. Replace one generic paragraph with a real, useful local detail. If nothing genuinely differs, consider consolidating rather than publishing a near-duplicate.
