# Don't Demo the Menu. Demo the Moment. — 13 November 2026

## Format
One five-slide Instagram carousel. Facebook receives one separate cover photo post only.

## Art direction
**Analog stage / director's cue sheet.** Warm paper, graphite, persimmon, sea-glass and lavender. Editorial serif/sans pairing; spotlight, hand-marked workflow line, open process nodes and a large conversation bubble. The visual story moves from stage/hero → discovery notes → workflow line → conversation bubble → proof checklist. No rounded cards, fake interface screenshots, stock photography or generated people.

## Grid and non-repetition audit
The connected Instagram media history shows recent posts on 6–12 November: content series, motion identity, channel fit, revision ownership, mobile thumb testing, pinned-post orientation, onboarding first-session clarity (8 Nov), Meta lead-form quality (9 Nov), founder-led marketing (10 Nov), ecommerce checkout UX (11 Nov), and SaaS pricing-page clarity (12 Nov). None in the available 14-day campaign archive uses a product-demo workflow framework.

Available archived visual sources reviewed: 9 Nov cobalt editorial receipt/lead-form concept; 11 Nov saturated-blue isometric checkout scene; 12 Nov warm off-white Swiss typographic grid. This campaign deliberately changes the focal device and visual rhythm: an analog spotlight, hand-marked discovery route, open process nodes, oversized speech bubble and coral typographic proof close. It does not reuse the olive/lime rounded-card template or the recent cobalt isometric checkout visual.

The live Instagram profile grid itself is login-gated; a read-only browser check was blocked by the Log In / Sign Up overlay. Therefore a complete visual thumbnail/contact-sheet inspection of the latest 9–12 live posts could not be completed. The archive SVGs and Instagram media metadata were reviewed instead. Do not claim a full live-grid visual audit.

## Audience and message
US B2B SaaS founders and product leads. One problem: product demos often become feature tours instead of showing a buyer's real workflow. One promise: a five-part framework to make the next demo easier to evaluate, not a guarantee of conversion.
